# Shoot runbook — one house, one card, 100 minutes

One-page print version: `SHOOT-SHEET.html` (keep the two in step).

For whoever is holding the camera. If you do something that isn't on this page,
write it down; the page gets amended after every job. The card is checked by
`retool card` before you leave — a `PART` station means shoot it again now.

## On arrival — 10 minutes

*2026-09-03:* **exteriors and the gimbal pass first, in daylight**, before
the first station: the walk-up, the front stills, the close and the grounds
moves while the light is right and the ISO does not swing. The tool finds
the pass by its motion, wherever it sits in the shoot.


1. Walk the house once with the agent or seller. Lights on everywhere, blinds
   and curtains open, fans off, TVs off, toilet lids down, counters clear.
   Say the trailer line while walking: we show enough to get a buyer off the
   couch and into the house, not every closet, laundry and garage. Said now,
   nobody calls back about the hallway bath (RESEARCH-video-edit.md §9).
   Ask which one thing sells the house; that is `Feature:` in `job.md`.
   *2026-09-06:* one exception to "lights on everywhere": a warm lamp
   standing in a daylit room goes off. The flash carries the stills; the
   clip is ambient-only and goes orange under 5000 K
   (EXPERIMENT-first-video-card.md, open).
2. Fix what the prep list missed: cars out of the driveway (yours on the
   street where no window sees it), bins out of sight, pet bowls, towels,
   chairs pushed in, beds squared. *2026-09-06:* and the things the book's
   author regretted most: throw rugs squared to the wall or rolled up, lamp
   cords behind the furniture, fridge magnets off, family photos and
   children's names out of frame, bottles out of the shower; a pantry or
   walk-in closet that sells gets its light on and its door open.
3. Decide the station list against the one below; note anything skipped and why.

## Per station — 3 minutes

1. **Stage** the frame: lights, blinds, chairs, cords, anything on the floor.
   *2026-09-06:* then the four edges of the frame: a chair back, a plant, a
   cord poking in is moved, or the camera pulls back a little. The book's
   regrets are mostly edges.
2. **Height** from the legs, never the tilt: **counter or furniture height**,
   about 90 cm, in most rooms; higher only under a very high ceiling.
   Exteriors: door/eye level. *2026-09-15, from the book; was ~120 cm keyed
   to the use-plane (kitchen counter+50, bath above the vanity, bedroom just
   above the mattress, 2026-09-06). Bath still needs to see into the basins;
   if counter height cannot, that is the one room to go up.*
   **Lens** at 13 mm; 11–12 only when the room needs it; never 10
   (RECIPE.md §6). **Compose** two-wall by default; one-point when the room
   calls for it; one wall as an option; anything highlighted, and every
   detail, on a third (RECIPE.md §6a). **Focus** AF-S, single point on the
   far point; on the subject when something near the lens must be sharp.
3. **Level** with the electronic level — roll AND pitch to zero. If you need
   more ceiling, raise the tripod; do not tilt. (The tool corrects small
   residuals; it will not rescue a tilted shot.) *2026-09-06:* then yaw until
   the dominant horizontal (bed foot, island edge, rug) runs parallel to the
   frame bottom. The tool fixes verticals, not this, and a slightly-off
   horizontal reads as careless; if it cannot be parallel, make the angle
   obviously deliberate.
4. **C1, press once.** Five frames fire on the 2 s timer. Do not touch the
   tripod until the last one.
5. **Check the middle frame**: 1/30 s or slower is the normal case indoors.
   *2026-09-06:* faster is fine in a daylit room, and will be the normal case
   on a daytime card. The rule guards against LED banding under the
   electronic shutter, which needs LED light to dominate, and then the room
   is dim. ISO stays 125 and the aperture stays f/6.4 either way
   (RECIPE.md §10). *2026-09-15:* small room with a near subject that must
   be sharp: f/8 on **both** banks, then back to f/6.4 at the next station.
6. **C2, press once.** Flash in **Manual** (*2026-09-15*, was TTL): at the
   first station fire one TTL shot, press TCM to lock the manual
   equivalent, then leave it for the house (RECIPE.md §3 has starting
   powers per room type). Flash head bounced 45–90° up and behind you, never at
   the wall you are shooting. Mirrors: shoot from an angle so neither the
   flash nor the camera is in it. *2026-09-06:* if the camera must be, put a
   door or a plain wall behind it so the reflection is cheap to remove, or
   drop the tripod behind the vanity.
7. **Video, one clip.** Flip to movie mode (4K 60p, F-Log2, 1/125, f/4,
   ISO auto). Hold 3 s still, pan three quarters of a frame to the
   destination (window, view, doorway into the next room) in about 3 s,
   hold 2 s still, stop. Everything is slowed 2× in the edit, so that is a
   6 s pan and 6 s of static on screen. No destination? Hold 3 s and stop.
   Never pan across a bright window.
   *2026-09-02:* pan in ~2.5 s rather than 3. At 95–100 BPM a 2.5 s pan
   fits two bars on screen and leaves room for the support rooms; a 3 s pan
   takes three bars and the planner drops dining and the grounds to stay
   under 110 s (PLAN-video-cut.md, step 2).
   *2026-09-03:* the hold-then-pan felt awkward on the first card; try **pan
   first, then hold 3 s at the end**. The planner follows the clip's own
   order either way. Big or oddly shaped room: **two or three pops**, within
   seconds; the tool composites them by lighten, so keep the flash out of
   reflective surfaces and yourself out of frame. *2026-09-15:* the extra
   pops go **off-camera**: X trigger on the shoe, V1 in hand, interval
   timer at 5 s for 3–4 frames, fire from a doorway or behind a wall at
   the dark part of the room, bounced or feathered off the nearest wall or
   ceiling, never a direct blast, and keep the wall next to you out of the
   beam. Every light on, except
   the warm lamp in a daylit room (On arrival, 1).
8. **The detail and the verticals.** *2026-09-03:* the 18-50 video static is
   dropped; the phone vertical is the video detail and the 18-50 is for
   still details only. Hero rooms: swap to the 18-50, zoom
   past 24 mm, frame one feature, C2 once for the still (horizontal), swap
   back. Then the phone, portrait, elbows in: two 4 s vertical clips, each
   with one small move (a tilt up the tile, a slide along the counter).
   Other rooms with a feature: one phone clip, no still. See "When to swap
   to the 18-50" below. *2026-09-06:* stage the detail frame too; the
   book's stove-hood close-up was spoiled by a butter dish.
9. Move on. Anything moving in the frame (curtains, fans, pets, people) — wait
   or turn it off; motion between brackets ruins the fusion.

## When to swap to the 18-50

The 10-18 is the default for every room wide. The 18-50 comes out exactly
four times per house, and the tool relies on the pattern:

- **Front exterior from the street** (station 2) when the lot allows — a
  full bracket station like any other.
- *2026-09-06:* **A front-door or porch detail** at station 1 when the
  build quality shows: C2 once past 24 mm, right after the facade's clip,
  so it files as `st01_d1`. The book's best-received detail was a custom
  front door; a porch column on an older house is the same idea.
- **One detail still in each hero room**: kitchen (range wall or island),
  living (fireplace or the view), primary bedroom (the headboard wall or
  window seat), primary bath (vanity and tile, or the shower). One per room,
  two at most; six per house is plenty.
- **Vertical clips, the social footage, on the phone**: two per hero
  room, one in every other room that has a feature, one of the facade. Ten
  to twelve per house, 4 s each with a small move. A 9:16 crop of a
  horizontal wide keeps a third of it, so the reels need footage shot
  vertical; the doorway moves and the pans crop acceptably and fill the
  rest. The phone is the practitioner standard for this: native portrait,
  no re-rig, windows handled. An L-bracket on the Fuji is the alternative
  if the one-camera look ever matters more than the ten seconds per clip it
  costs.
- **Never for a room wide**, and never in a bare room.

How to shoot a detail so the tool files it correctly: stay at the station,
right after the video clip, so the timestamp ties it to the room. Zoom
past 24 mm — anything longer than the room's bracket lens is read as a
detail; at 18 mm it is ambiguous. Press C2 once (the flash carries a detail,
no bracket). Then the verticals on the phone: portrait, 4 s each, one
small move, shot in station order so the sequence is right; the tool
matches each phone clip to its room by picture, so the phone's clock need
not agree with the camera's. What makes
a detail: a material, a fixture, a view — never furniture, never the toaster.
Verticals still plumb; if the feature needs a downward tilt, tilt less and
step back.

Output: `stNN_d1.jpg` next to the room's wide, listed under it on the
scorecard, placed right after it by `deliver`.

## Gimbal pass — 10 minutes, the connective set only

The tripod clips carry every room. The gimbal exists to answer "where am I"
between rooms, so it shoots only the moves that go through a door, up a
stair or out a threshold. Shoot 60p, walk at a normal pace with bent knees
and heel-toe steps; the slow motion comes from the edit. Every move begins
and ends on 2 s of stillness. *2026-09-03:* the walk between the two
stills is **about 4 s**; longer walks are cut into. The 10-18 at 10–12 mm,
not the 18-50. Shoot the pass first, in daylight (see On arrival). Shoot them in this order, one clip each:

1. **Walk-up**: start behind a post or hedge, 2 s still, walk to the door, 2 s still.
2. **Entry**: one continuous move through the door into the living room;
   end on the feature or the window, never on a wall.
3. **Each hallway or stair**: head-on, level, one clip per hallway. It's the
   neutral shot that lets the edit change direction.
4. **Primary suite**: push in from the door, then look back toward the door
   or the terrace. *v1 had no look-back.*
5. **Out to the grounds**: dark interior → bright exterior is the film's
   punctuation; walk it in one take if you can. *2026-09-06:* end outside,
   2 s still with the yard in frame; v1 ended at the door.
6. **Close**: the only pull-back in the film, from the best exterior angle.

Never tilt to keep verticals — lower the camera instead. Why the gimbal
list is short: `RESEARCH-video-edit.md`.

## Drone — 15 minutes, right after the gimbal pass (*2026-09-29*)

Still in daylight, cars and bins already gone. Aerials show what the ground
can't: lot size and privacy, where the house sits on the lot and in the
neighbourhood, outbuildings, parking, the water (Castle, five or six per
house, "a final trip around the home"). Settings: RECIPE.md §5a. Skip the
whole section under a no-fly rule and write why in `job.md`.

**Stills, one AEB press per shot:**

| # | shot | height | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | front, ¾ from the stronger corner | 20–40 ft, about roofline | the aerial cover; driveway as a leading line into the house, house on a third |
| A2 | front, straight on, symmetrical | 30–50 ft | front wall parallel to the horizon; Castle's "better" over an oblique of clashing walls |
| A3 | backyard and rear | 30–60 ft | yard depth, fence line, patio or pool |
| A4 | the side that shows something unique | 30–60 ft | garage layout, outbuilding, carport, boat garage; skip if nothing |
| A5 | context | 150–400 ft, toward the draw | lake, skyline, greenway, entrance; lot size and privacy. Nothing to point at: skip |
| A6 | overhead, camera straight down | 100–150 ft | optional; the lot's shape at a glance |

Composition, every shot: horizon on a third, not the middle; the house's
dominant wall parallel to the horizon (yaw the drone, then the tool's
verticals pass cleans up the rest); no confusing oblique of several wall
planes. Tilt only as far as the shot needs.

**Video, Cine mode, 2 s still at both ends, the move about 5–8 s** (slowed
2× in the edit like everything else):

1. **V1 rising reveal**: from low over the front walk, climb to roofline
   or above, tilting down as it rises. The opener.
2. **V2 orbit**: half circle around the house at 30–50 ft, point of
   interest on the roof ridge. Middle of the exterior run.
3. **V3 push-in**: straight toward the front door at roofline height,
   camera level. The hand-off to the entry.
4. **V4 pull-back and rise**: reverse of V1, ending on the context (lake,
   neighbourhood). The closer.

The cut already has an `aerial_view` exterior slot; until the tool reads
the drone card, copy the stills and clips into the job by hand.

- Any `PART` station → shoot it again now. Any `REVIEW` deviation you can't
  explain → shoot that station again now.

  the list above, station clips against the station count, and phone
  verticals: eight or more.

## Seller prep list (send at booking, copy verbatim)

> To get the most from the shoot, before we arrive please: turn on every light
> and replace any dead bulbs; open all blinds and curtains; turn off ceiling
> fans and TVs; clear kitchen and bathroom counters; put toilet lids down and
> hang fresh towels; make all beds; put away pet beds, bowls and toys; roll up
> small rugs or square them to the walls; tuck lamp cords behind the
> furniture; take magnets and notes off the fridge; put away family photos
> and anything with a child's name on it; empty the shower of bottles; move
> cars out of the driveway and bins out of sight; tidy the porch and the yard
> (hoses, toys, furniture cushions). We photograph the house as we find it —
> what's out at 10:00 is what's in the listing.
